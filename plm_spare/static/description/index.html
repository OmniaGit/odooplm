Spare Parts

Overview

The spare parts catalogue your engineering data already contains.

After sales is where a manufacturer loses the most time on the least interesting work: rebuilding, by hand, a spare parts list that the engineering department already drew. A service technician asks for the bushing of a bearing unit, and somebody opens the assembly drawing, counts the balloons, looks up the part numbers, and retypes them into a document that is out of date the next time the product is revised.

This module builds that catalogue from the data you already have. The spare bill of material lives next to the manufacturing one, on the same product, and the manual is printed from it — not maintained beside it.

What it does

A spare BOM per product

A dedicated bill of material of type spare, created from the manufacturing one in a single action, keeping only the parts that are actually replaced in service. Both live on the same product, so a revision never leaves them out of step.

Parts flagged where they are

A product can be marked as a spare part once and be recognised as such everywhere — in the BOM lines, in the searches, in the views. No parallel list to keep aligned.

The manual prints itself

The Spare Parts Manual is generated on demand: a cover with the product and its picture, then the drawings you flagged for service, assembled into one PDF. For a whole product tree or for a single assembly, on the spot, always from the current revision.

Drawings you choose

Each engineering document carries a Used for Spare flag: the assembly sheets that help a technician go in, not the whole archive. The balloon on the sheet and the row of the spare BOM speak the same language.
